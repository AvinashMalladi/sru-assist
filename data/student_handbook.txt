1. ABOUT SR UNIVERSITY 
SR University (SRU) is a State Private University located in Warangal, Telangana. Spread across a 170-
acre verdant campus, the university is home to over 11,000 students and more than 1,200 faculty and 
staff, with six distinct schools. 
Vision 
• To accelerate the transformation and advancement of the regional innovation ecosystem 
through academic excellence, industry relevance, and social responsibility. 
Mission 
• Develop technically competent, industry-ready, and socially responsible leaders. 
• Undertake groundbreaking research and actively disseminate its outcomes. 
• Foster collaborations with industry, government, and non -profit organizations to serve the 
broader community.

2.1. Student Registration and University Identity 
Enrollment Number Framework 
Enrollment and registration mark the formal commencement of a student’s academic journey at the 
University. The process, from admission to final enrollment, is structured to ensure transparency, 
accuracy, and systematic academic record management, as illustrated in Flowchart 1.1. 
Upon successful admission, a student’s enrollment remains provisional until the University officially 
assigns a unique Institutional Enrollment Number. To ensure administrative accuracy and avoid 
duplication of records, the University follows a structured enrollment identification framework. 
Each student is allotted a unique 10 -digit Enrollment Number representing the Year of Admission, 
School Code, Degree Code, Program Code, and serial number.  
Component Description 
Year of Admission Admission Year 
School Code School Identifier 
Degree Code Degree Type 
Program Code Program Identifier 
Serial Number Unique Student Number 
Example: For B. Tech CSE 
2 3 0 3 A 5 1 0 0 1 
Year of Admission School Code Degree Code Program Code Serial Number 
University Identification Card 
Every student shall be issued a University Identification Card containing the Enrollment Number, 
student photograph, name, and other essential details. The ID card serves as the official identity 
credential within the University campus. 
Students are required to carry and wear the ID card at all times while on campus and during 
participation in external academic, sports, cultural, or official University activities. 
In case of loss or damage, a duplicate ID card may be issued upon payment of the prescribed fee as 
per University policy.

2.4. Mentoring and Academic Advising Framework 
The University recognizes mentoring and academic advising as integral components of student 
success, academic progression, and holistic development. Each student is assigned a faculty mentor at 
the time of enrollment to provide continuous academic guidance and institutional support throughout 
the program. 
Mentoring System 
The mentoring framework is designed to support students in their academic, personal, and 
professional development. Faculty mentors act as the primary point of contact for guidance, support, 
and student engagement. 
Mentor Responsibilities 
Support students in academic and personal development. 
Facilitate timely communication and academic updates. 
Guide students in addressing academic challenges and identifying growth opportunities. 
Promote student engagement and overall well-being. 
Mentoring Engagement 
At least one individual mentor–mentee interaction shall be conducted each semester. 
At least one group mentoring session shall be organized each semester. 
Students are encouraged to seek guidance from their mentors whenever required. 
Academic Advising 
Academic advising complements the mentoring process by helping students make informed 
decisions regarding their academic progression and future career pathways. 
Academic Advising Areas 
Course Registration and Academic Planning. 
Goal Setting and Academic Progress Monitoring. 
Internships, Research, and Experiential Learning Opportunities. 
Higher Education and Career Development Pathways.

2.7. Student Feedback and Escalation Framework 
Open Door Policy 
The University follows a transparent and student-centric Open Door Policy that encourages students 
to share suggestions, concerns, and feedback regarding academic and campus -related matters. 
Students may communicate with faculty members, administrative off ices, or University leadership 
through meetings, official emails, or scheduled appointments. Students are encouraged to follow the 
prescribed communication hierarchy for routine matters before approaching higher authorities. The 
University values construct ive feedback as an important component of continuous institutional 
improvement and student engagement. 
Feedback Mechanism 
The Office of Dean Academics collects student feedback before Mid Sem and End Sem examinations 
through the University’s online feedback system. Students, parents, and alumni may also submit 
feedback and suggestions through the official communication channel at feedback@sru.edu.in. The 
University remains committed to continuously improving the overall learning and campus experience 
through active stakeholder participation. 
Student Grievance Redressal Cell 
The Student Grievance Redressal Cell functions as the designated institutional mechanism for 
addressing student grievances and concerns related to academic, administrative, and campus matters. 
Students may submit grievances through the official email ID stu.grievance@sru.edu.in or contact the 
University Help Desk. All grievances shall be reviewed and resolved within a reasonable timeframe 
based on the nature and severity of the issue.

2.9 Student Clubs and Professional Societies 
Clubs at the university provide a platform for students to develop talents, explore interests, and 
pursue passions. Students are encouraged to join at least one club per semester to expand their skills 
and experiences. 
The clubs aim to identify and nurture students’ potential, supporting their academic and personal 
growth through engaging activities. Open to all, these clubs organize events year -round, enriching 
students’ social, cultural, and academic experiences. They serve as a hub for learning, networking, and 
socializing beyond the classroom. For more details on club activities, visit the university website. 
The following clubs are active for the academic year 2025-26 onwards. 
University Level Clubs: 
Garden Club:  The Garden Club is committed to restoring a sustainable ecosystem while fostering 
interest in horticulture, gardening, and garden therapy. It encourages the beautification of the college 
campus, inspiring students to take an active role in environmental conservation. Through floral design 
and greenery initiatives, the club cultivates a passion for gardening and promotes civic engagement in 
sustainable practices. 
Organic Farming Club: The Organic Farming Club is a student-led initiative that promotes sustainable 
agriculture and environmentally conscious practices within the university community. The club serves 
as a platform for students, faculty, and staff who are passionate about orga nic cultivation, food 
security, and ecological balance. 
Yoga Club:  This club promotes confidence, enthusiasm, and well -being in a non -competitive 
environment. It enhances physical and emotional health, improves focus, concentration, and memory, 
and helps ease stress and anxiety. Through regular practice, students develop better posture, patience, 
and self-awareness, fostering relaxation, mindfulness, and inner balance. 
Literary Master Communicators Club:  This club enhances communication skills across diverse 
contexts, fostering critical thinking, confidence, and performance abilities for inter -collegiate events 
and contests. 
Artistic ( Painting and Sketching ) Club: This club nurtures creativity, confidence, and artistic 
exploration through various painting and sketching techniques. Students engage with historical and 
contemporary artistic practices, expanding their ideas and personal styles. By developing their own 
work, they cultivate self -expression, innovative thinking, and design skills, fostering a deeper 
appreciation for visual arts. 
Community Service Center: This center encourages students to identify community issues, engage in 
problem-solving, and develop a strong sense of civic responsibility. It fosters leadership, teamwork, 
and social awareness, helping students apply their knowledge to real-world challenges. Through active 
participation, members gain skills in mobilizing community efforts, responding to emergencies, and 
promoting social harmony, contributing to national integration and collective well-being. 
Martial Arts Club:  The Martial Arts Club promotes fitness, self -confidence, and discipline through 
structured training. Members develop physical strength, mental resilience, and teamwork, while 
learning both defensive and offensive techniques in a safe and supportive environment. 
Adventure Club: This club offers students exciting opportunities to explore nature, build resilience, 
and develop leadership through activities like trekking, camping, and rock climbing. It encourages 
teamwork, eco-awareness, and personal growth beyond the classroom. 
Hiking Club: This club encourages students to explore local trails, stay active, and connect with nature. 
It promotes fitness, mindfulness, and outdoor camaraderie. 
Cultural Club:  The Cultural Club is dedicated to celebrating the diverse traditions and festivals 
observed across different communities. It serves as a platform for students to embrace cultural 
heritage through various events, performances, and interactive activities. The club organizes festivals,

3.4. Honours and Minor Degree Framework 
Honours Degree 
The Honours Degree framework enables academically motivated students to pursue advanced 
learning in their discipline through additional specialized coursework. 
• Students must maintain the prescribed minimum CGPA at the time of registration and award 
of the Honours Degree.  
• A minimum of 16 credits shall be earned through approved advanced courses.  
• These credits must be completed through overloading during regular or Summer Semesters.  
• Courses completed under the Honours framework shall not overlap with the core curriculum 
requirements of the parent program.  
• Additional registration fees shall apply as per University policy.  
• These courses can be also taken in summer semester. 
Minor Degree 
The Minor Degree framework provides students the opportunity to acquire multidisciplinary 
knowledge in a secondary domain outside their parent discipline. 
• Students shall complete a minimum of 16 credits from an approved Minor course basket.  
• Registration through course overloading is mandatory.  
• Minor courses shall be distinct from the core curriculum of the parent program.  
• Additional fees shall be applicable for the registered Minor courses as per university policy. 
• These courses can be also taken in summer semester.

Closed Book and Open Book Examinations

Closed Book Examination

Open Book Examination

examination. Such examinations are designed to evaluate application of knowledge, critical thinking, 
analysis, interpretation, and problem-solving rather than memorization. 
The mode of examination shall be determined by the University from time to time and notified 
through the approved examination schedule.

4.1.  Student Code of Conduct 
The University is committed to fostering a culture rooted in responsibility, inclusivity, and academic 
integrity. The university encourages students to embrace the freedom of learning while upholding 
ethical behaviour, mutual respect, and social accountability. The follo wing Code of Conduct outlines 
the expected standards of behaviour for all students across academic, digital, and social domains. 
Applicability of the Code 
The code shall be applicable to all students admitted to the university and including any academic 
program, activity or event conducted by the university. It is the duty and responsibility of each student 
from the date of admission to get acquainted with all the rules and code of conduct. All students are 
required to strictly adhere to this code and this code would be binding on and enforceable on them. 
Student Responsibilities 
It shall be the responsibility of the student, 
To produce Identity Card whenever asked by any official/staff of the university. 
To foster and maintain a vibrant academic, intellectual, cultural and social atmosphere which is 
consistent with the objectives of the university. 
To access all educational opportunities and benefits available at the university and make good use of 
them to prosper academically. 
To respect the laws of the country, human rights and to always conduct in a responsible and dignified 
manner. To report any violation of this code to the functionaries of the university. 
Student joining in any academic program of the university will have to give an undertaking to the effect 
that the student will comply with the provisions envisaged in this code in letter and spirit and even if 
it is not given, they will be bound by the provisions of this code. 
General Conduct Expectations 
Respect and Inclusivity: Students shall embrace diversity and uphold mutual respect, regardless of 
background, culture, or belief. 
Personal Appearance and Behaviour: Maintain decency in attire and uphold decorum in all university

43 
 
spaces, both physical and digital. 
Civic Responsibility: Always demonstrate courtesy and consideration toward faculty, staff, peers, and 
the campus community. 
Social Media Use: Posting defamatory or inappropriate content, including reels or images against the 
university or its members, is prohibited and subject to disciplinary action. 
Digital Citizenship: Students are expected to use university -provided digital platforms ethically and 
refrain from creating unauthorized groups or sharing misleading content. 
SRU discourages the use of single-use plastics on campus to support environmental sustainability. 
4.2. Academic Integrity and Ethical Conduct 
Students must uphold the highest standards of honesty and responsibility in academic pursuits. 
Academic dishonesty undermines the core values of SRU and will not be tolerated. 
All submissions, assessments, and research must be the student’s own. Collaborative work is 
permitted only when explicitly authorized. 
Plagiarism: Students must properly acknowledge all external ideas, images, data, and digital content, 
including content generated by AI tools. 
Cheating and Fabrication: Use of unauthorized aids, impersonation, falsifying records, or tampering 
with academic documents is prohibited. 
Respect for Evaluation Processes: Attempting to influence grading decisions or disrupting others' 
academic work is unethical and subject to penalty. 
Discipline and Prohibited Conduct 
Substance Abuse: SRU enforces a strict zero-tolerance policy toward alcohol, drugs, tobacco, and any 
other prohibited substances on campus. 
Harassment and Discrimination: Any form of harassment —verbal, physical, emotional, sexual, or 
cyber is forbidden. 
Possession of Weapons: Carrying weapons or dangerous items on campus is strictly prohibited. 
Dress and Identification: Students are expected to wear formal  attire on Monday and Tuesday or 
decent attire during academic hours and must always carry their university ID card. 
Mobile Phone Policy 
Permitted Use: Mobile phones must be used responsibly. 
Prohibited Areas: Usage is banned in classrooms, laboratories, and examination halls. 
Misuse: Use of mobile phones for recording or sharing unauthorized content may result in confiscation 
and disciplinary measures. 
Banned Content: Accessing or distributing offensive, pornographic, or illegal material using university 
networks is punishable. 
Disciplinary Measures and Consequences 
Violations of the Code of Conduct may lead to one or more of the following. 
Formal warning or reprimand. 
Suspension from specific classes, hostels, or activities. 
Cancellation of examinations or assignments. 
Financial penalties. 
Rustication or permanent expulsion. 
Withdrawal of scholarships or other academic privileges. 
All decisions will be made by the Disciplinary Committee based on the nature and gravity of the 
offense. 
Research and Publication Ethics 
Students engaged in research are expected to adhere to international standards of academic ethics. 
Responsible Research: Ensure ethical treatment of human and animal subjects and responsible 
disposal of research materials. 
 
Publishing Guidelines 
Avoid duplicate submissions or simultaneous submissions to multiple journals.

4.4. Punishment Provisions in cases of Ragging 
Any student or group of students found guilty of ragging on campus or off campus shall be liable to 
one or more of the following punishments:  
Debarring from appearing in any mid exam/ university examination or withholding results. 
Suspension from attending classes and academic privileges. 
Withdrawing scholarships and other benefits. 
Suspension from the college for a period of one month. 
Cancellation of admission. 
Debarring from representing the institution in any national or international meet, tournament, youth 
festival, etc. 
Suspension/expulsion from the hostel. 
Rustication from the institution for periods varying from 1 to 4 semesters or equivalent period. 
Expulsion from the institution and consequent debarring from admission to any other institution. 
Fine ranging between Rs. 25,000/- and Rupees 1 Lakh.  
Imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years or with fine which may extend to ten 
thousand rupees or with both. 
Collective punishment - When the students committing or abetting the crime of ragging are not 
identified, the institution shall resort to collective punishment as a deterrent to ensure community 
pressure on the potential raggers. 
4.5. Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH) in Workplace 
University is committed to providing a safe and respectful environment for all members of its 
academic community, including staff and students. This Sexual Harassment Policy aims to prevent and 
address any form of sexual harassment within the university setting. It applies to all staff, students, 
and visitors and reflects our commitment to fostering a culture of respect, dignity, and inclusivity. 
Any student who experiences or witnesses sexual harassment is strongly encouraged to report the 
incident promptly to the appropriate authorities. Reporting can be done through multiple channels,

Evaluation and Results 
Evaluation of students, examinations and grades 
The evaluation of the student in a course shall be based on his /her performance in various kinds of

The detailed course outlines in each course shall be prepared by the teacher(s) concerned  in

to them. This shall not apply forboth the final theory and practical examinations. 
Mid-semester examinations 
There shall be one mid-semester examination to be conducted by the teacher offering the course after 
50% of the working days are over in a semester. The duration for the mid-semester examination shall 
be for one and half hours and the final theory examination shall be two and half hours. 
The marks allotted for mid -semester and semester final theory examinations shall be 50 and 100, 
respectively. Ordinarily no condonation for absence of mid -semester examination shall be given.

examinations in view of their participation in the inter -University and National Sports, Literary and 
Cultural events etc., along with the students who genuinely prevented from taking examination in the 
case of serious illness or accident or any other case. This repeat examination shall be held within two 
weeks from the date of examinations so missed and  shall be a common examination for all such 
students. 
Unless a student appears for the mid-semester examination, he/she shall not be permitted to appear 
for the semester final theory and practical examinations in the course concerned. The special re -
examination shall be conducted as per the time to be fixed by the Dean within two weeks from the 
last date of mid semester examinations. The weightage of marks  allotted for mid semester and 
semester final theory examination shall be as per VDeans Committee recommendations. 
Semester final examinations

in the theory portion shall be of two and half hour’s duration. It shall be the responsibility of the 
University to conduct the theory portion of the final examination. Practical examinations shall be 
conducted by the respective colleges. The students shall be given two preparation holidays (including 
the public holiday) before the commencement of the semester final theory examinations. 
Answer scripts of semester final theory examinations are evaluated through common spot valuation 
system year of study wise. On the last day of semester final examinations, the Dean shall send all the 
sealed answer scripts to the selected center where they are coded, before distribution for valuation.

University from time to time for each semester.

Plagiarism Policy 
Research excellence in the areas of Science and Technology, the Humanities, and Social 
Responsibilities is a goal at university. We demonstrate our dedication to interdisciplinary study by 
continuing to conduct both applied and fundamental studies of lasting significance. By adhering to the 
strictest scholarly norms, University encourages study across all its primary and interdisciplinary fields. 
Data recording, analysis, and utilization are emphasized. Students at University are encouraged to 
engage in cri tical thinking. Advancing scientific understanding for future generations requires going 
beyond what is taught in schools and what is published in academic journals. The institution's 
philosophy is predicated on the idea that creating research methods to e xpand knowledge and 
developing the full person is essential to fostering a culture of research. SRU will take necessary steps 
to adapt the best practices and protocols to promote research integrity among its students through 
all measures. 
The University acknowledges that the free exchange of ideas is essential to academic endeavours, as 
it is often only through discussion with others that one can completely process information or 
crystallize an elusive concept. Consequently, students are commonly motivated to engage in dialogues 
with their teachers and peers regarding their courses, research, and even homework. These types of 
discussions and debates represent, in some respects, the essence of academic life. Nonetheless, it is 
essential for a ll academics to explicitly acknowledge when they have relied on or incorporated the 
work of others. 
Plagiarism definition 
According to SR University, plagiarism is "to offer work or ideas from another source as one's own, 
with or without authorization of the source author(s), directly by verbatim copying or by usage of any 
AI software" (i.e., with or without permission from the original author). In certain cases, authorization 
might be provided for the usage of other sources through written permission may not be considered 
as plagiarism. 
Self-plagiarism, a form of plagiarism, can also be committed by reusing one's own work which is 
published in a source without proper citation to that source. It is considered plagiarism in educational 
content to use someone else's idea or phrase without properly attributi ng the source of the work. It 
makes no difference if the source is an accomplished writer, another pupil, a web page without 
distinguished authorship, a website that sells research papers, or anyone else as well, whether done 
knowingly or unintentionally, claiming credit for someone else's work is considered as a form of 
academic theft and is despicable in all educational environments. 
An act of "plagiarism" is any use of words or concepts from another writer without giving proper credit 
to them. Plagiarism is the unacknowledged use of another author's words, ideas, or structure in one's 
own work, whether those words, ideas, or structure are published or not.  
Misrepresentation: Research and publications are misunderstood; there has been fabrication and 
distortion of research material published in book chapters, journals, and conference proceedings.

Redundant submission: Not getting permission from your teacher before submitting work that has 
been submitted in whole or in significant part for another class or course that is already used for 
academic purposes. 
Forms of plagiarism 
Verbatim: Quotations must be marked with quotation marks or indentation and fully cited. The reader 
must know which parts are your own and which are borrowed. 
Cut and paste: This type is defined when a person copies and paste the content from sources including 
internet, the list of sources must include references to Internet -derived information. Since Internet 
content is less likely to have been peer-reviewed, it should be carefully assessed. 
Paraphrasing: Without proper citation, replicating someone else's work by altering a few words and 
rearranging the sequence in which they appear or by closely following the framework of their 
argument to develop another related content. 
Collusion: This can include things like working together on a project without permission, failing to 
properly credit third -party assistance, or failing to adhere to guidelines for working in groups. You 
should know exactly how much teamwork is allowed and what respon sibilities fall solely on one's 
shoulders. 
Inaccurate citation: Always use the standard format for citations in the area, one works in. Author 
should cite the sources not only at the end of the paper (in a bibliography), but also within the text 
itself (in a footnote). Also, one should not include books, articles, or w ebsites in the bibliography or 
references that you did not consult. 
Failure to acknowledge assistance: One should provide proper credit to any source who helped you 
while creating your work, whether they are classmates, lab assistants, or anyone else.  
Auto-plagiarism (Identical submission): One must not develop or submit an assessment related work 
that is already submitted either in partial or full, for a course or for any other qualification, from this 
university or other, electronically, or manually.
